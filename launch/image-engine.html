Generating our stills in bulk

You asked whether ChatGPT image generation can be automated — an extension, a repo, a Codex trick, or a "ChatGPT Multi" like the Veo tool. Short answer: it can be automated, and you still shouldn't. The reason is not the automation. It's that we are almost certainly comparing ChatGPT against the wrong engine on our own side.

The verdict

Veo Flow Multi has been generating with Imagen 4 this whole time.

The image-model dropdown in our own tool lists three options and the first one — the default — is labelled "Imagen 4 (best)". It is not the best. It is the oldest of the three. Google's current top image model, Nano Banana Pro, is the third option in that same dropdown, and unless you changed it by hand every image we've made came from Imagen 4. That is the realism gap you've been feeling — not "Veo can't do realism".

Two things follow from that, and they cost nothing to check:

1 · The gap may already be closed

Nano Banana Pro is a genuinely current model, it is already wired into our tool, and image generation there costs 0 credits — measured live by the tool's own balance-delta readout, across ~10 pooled accounts. Switching the dropdown is a 2-second change.

2 · It is the only model that promises our exact problem

Google documents Nano Banana Pro as accepting "up to 5 images of characters to maintain character consistency". We have a repertory cast of exactly five — Aloo, Tamatar, Pyaz, Matar aunty, Mirchi. No other model publishes a character-slot count at all, and OpenAI ships an explicit warning that it "may occasionally struggle to maintain visual consistency for recurring characters."

Nothing here is a decision yet. Step one is a 20-minute A/B you run yourself, below. If Nano Banana Pro still loses to ChatGPT on our look, we fall back to the paid API route in §4 — which is still not the ChatGPT web app.

What I'd build — the Veo Flow Multi image upgrade

Three changes, in order of value. Nothing here needs a new app; you already own the hard part (auth harvesting, reCAPTCHA minting, account pooling, the job queue).

① Fix the dropdown minutes

Make Nano Banana Pro the default and relabel it. The current "Imagen 4 (best)" label is actively misleading us and is the root cause of this whole question.

② Reference images on the image path the real work

The image call currently sends prompt + model + aspect + count and nothing else. The video path already knows how to attach reference images. Port that shape onto image generation so each render carries the five locked cast portraits. This is what turns a batch tool into a character-consistent batch tool.

③ Prompt list → images → I2V, one run nice to have

We already paste 8 image prompts and 8 i2v prompts per short. Chain them: generate the stills, auto-map each to its i2v prompt, fire the video batch. Removes the manual hand-off between our two copy-blocks.

One real limitation to accept up front. Flow images come out at 1376×768 native and image upscaling inside Flow is a known dead end — the endpoint body is undocumented and our earlier attempt shipped best-effort only. For 9:16 shorts that is a low pixel count going into Veo. It has not visibly hurt shorts 1–18, because Veo re-renders and the video path does have working 1080p/4K upscaling — but if the A/B shows softness, that is the reason, and route 2 (which does 2K natively) is the fix.

Why it still fails for us

The quota, not the tech

OpenAI publishes no image limit for Plus — I checked every help article. What users report through 2026 is a few dozen images and then a cooldown measured in hours, with failed and content-refused generations still counting against you. Those community numbers are unsourced and I could not corroborate them, so treat them as a warning rather than a figure. But no one anywhere reports pulling hundreds a day.

The terms are explicit

OpenAI's Terms of Use forbid users to "automatically or programmatically extract data or Output" and to "circumvent any rate limits or restrictions." Every one of these tools markets randomised delays "to stay under rate limits" — squarely the second clause.

And the risk isn't the ban

I found zero first-hand reports of a permanent ban for UI automation — the observed penalty is temporary lockouts and repeated human-verification prompts. The real hazard is that when OpenAI's classifiers do flag an account, 2026's appeals have been auto-rejected. It's a small chance of an unappealable loss, for a route that can't hit our volume anyway.

Also worth knowing: Google publishes a Chrome Web Store policy change taking effect 1 Aug 2026 that disallows extensions "designed to circumvent safety guardrails, usage restrictions, or other protective measures implemented by AI-powered services." Whether these two extensions are read as covered is not stated anywhere — but the category is in the blast radius, six days out.

"Can Codex generate too?" — yes, and here's the catch

This was your sharpest question, and the answer is a genuine yes. It's just bounded in a way OpenAI states outright.

What's true

Codex has built-in image generation. Per OpenAI's docs it "uses gpt-image-2 and counts toward your general Codex usage limits" — i.e. it is paid for by the Plus subscription, not by API billing. Image generation is available on Plus (not on Free). Codex runs headlessly via codex exec and has official TypeScript and Python SDKs, so looping it over a prompt list is trivial.

What kills it

Two sentences from the same page. "Image generations use included limits 3–5× faster on average than similar turns without image generation." And then, plainly: "For larger batches, set OPENAI_API_KEY in your environment and ask ChatGPT to generate images through the API so API pricing applies." OpenAI is telling you that at batch volume you are meant to be on route 2.

Also dead, so nobody wastes a week on it: Sora. The consumer app and website were discontinued 26 Apr 2026, and the Sora API shuts down 24 Sep 2026 with no replacement model listed. Any plan that routes through Sora has two months of life.
And the premise worth retiring: there is no "Veo 3 image generation". Flow's stills come from Imagen 4, Nano Banana 2, or Nano Banana Pro — three separate models, one dropdown. Veo is only the video half.

What I could not verify — read before you spend

Flow image generation being credit-free is our measurement, not Google's promise. Google's published Flow credit table lists only video operations and omits images entirely — that is an argument from silence, not a stated zero. Our own tool reads the real balance delta after each batch and has recorded 0 for images. Re-check the delta on the first Nano Banana Pro run before we lean on "free".
"gpt-image-2 is the model inside ChatGPT" is a strong inference, not a sourced fact. No OpenAI page says it. The evidence is that the API snapshot is dated the same day ChatGPT Images 2.0 launched, and that OpenAI's deprecation table retires the chatgpt-image-latest alias and names gpt-image-2 as its replacement. Good enough to plan on; not good enough to quote.
The ChatGPT Plus image cap is genuinely unpublished. Every number circulating online traces to content farms. I am relying on the shape of community reports, not a figure.
Nobody has published a controlled test of character drift over hundreds of renders. The only measured curve found stops at 20 images, where face similarity had already decayed ~10%. Every vendor's consistency claim — Google's five character slots included — is a marketing claim until we test it on our own five.
Google's "5 characters" is Nano Banana Pro specifically, and the phrasing counts images of characters, drawn from a 14-image total budget — not necessarily five distinct people rendered perfectly. Our cast is exactly five, so this is right on the documented edge. Test it before we build the whole pipeline on it.
